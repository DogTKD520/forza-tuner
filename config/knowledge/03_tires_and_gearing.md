# Tires
Tire pressure controls the shape of the contact patch. Too high and the patch becomes small and round (less grip, more skating). Too low and the tire deforms badly under load. Higher pressures make the car more responsive. Lower pressures are more forgiving. Very low pressures (20 psi) increase mechanical grip but make the car feel unresponsive and cost straight-line speed. Very high pressures (40 psi+) give better launch and top speed but cost cornering grip.
- Slick: 28 – 32.5 psi
- Semi-Slick: 27 – 29.5 psi
- Stock / Street / Rally: 24 – 26.5 psi
- Off-Road: 15.5 – 21 psi

Slicks want more air because grippier tires have stiffer sidewalls.

# Gearing
Lower final drive (numerically smaller) = taller gears, higher top speed, slower acceleration. Higher final drive = shorter gears, more acceleration, lower top speed.
Adjust the final drive only; leave individual gear ratios at default.
